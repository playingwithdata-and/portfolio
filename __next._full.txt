## Overview

## Overview

A client was spending 70% of their digital ad budget on one platform because "that's where the audience is." The data said otherwise. I pulled campaign-level performance data across five platforms, calculated ROAS at the channel, campaign, and creative level, and surfaced reallocation opportunities worth 40% more return.

## What I did

- **Data extraction** — pulled spend, impression, click, and conversion data from Meta Ads, Google Ads, TikTok Ads, LinkedIn Ads, and programmatic
- **ROAS modeling** — calculated return on ad spend at three levels: platform, campaign, and individual creative
- **Attribution analysis** — mapped conversion paths to understand how channels influenced each other
- **Budget recommendation** — built a reallocation model that maintained total spend while shifting budget to higher-ROAS channels

## Key findings

- One underfunded channel had 3.2x the ROAS of the top-budget channel
- Video creatives under 15 seconds consistently outperformed longer formats
- Weekend campaigns had 22% lower CPA than weekday campaigns across all platforms

## Tech

- **Excel** — Pivot Tables, Power Query, data modeling
- **Google Analytics** — conversion tracking and attribution
- **Looker Studio** — client-facing dashboard

## Result

Recommended budget shift increased projected ROAS by 40% without increasing total spend. Client adopted the recommendation in Q2.

## Overview

An e-commerce client had three years of transaction data sitting in their database with no one analyzing it. I wrote a series of SQL queries to segment customers, identify revenue trends, and find product affinities. The results changed who they thought their "best" customers were.

## What I did

- **Data cleaning** — identified and handled duplicates, missing values, and inconsistent formatting across 500K+ rows
- **Customer segmentation** — RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis to group customers into actionable segments
- **Revenue trend analysis** — monthly and quarterly revenue decomposition by product category and customer segment
- **Product affinity analysis** — market basket analysis to identify products frequently purchased together

## Key findings

- The top 8% of customers generated 42% of revenue — but they weren't the customers the marketing team had been targeting
- Two product categories showed strong affinity (purchased together 34% of the time) but were never cross-promoted
- Q4 revenue spike was driven almost entirely by repeat customers, not new acquisitions

## Tech

- **SQL** — CTEs, window functions, joins, aggregation
- **Excel** — final visualization and reporting
- **Tableau** — customer segment dashboards

## Result

Marketing team shifted their retention strategy to focus on the actual high-value segment. Cross-promotion campaign for the two affinity categories launched the following quarter.
